Project 3: Ising Model

In our `figures` folder, there are some of the initial images comparing the Energy to sweeps and Magnetization per spin to sweeps. We later updated these with mean values, lines for selected burn-ins, and the associated autocorrelations.
`Ising_3DCube.py` contains the 3D lattice model that produced graphs depicting the Energy vs. Sweeps and Magnetization per spin vs. Sweeps. Like the images in `figures`, the plots include mean values, labeled burn-ins, and calculated $\tau$ values. The values for temperature and lattice size are user-controlled and were manually edited to study the behavior at different temperatures for a 10x10x10 lattice.
`critical_temp.py` references `ising.py` (Metropolis-Hastings method) to plot Magnetization versus Temperature. This involved looping through a range of temperatures and calculating the average absolute magnetization from our Metropolis-Hastings algorithm.
`ising.py` was our initial Metropolis-Hastings algorithm and creates plots for Energy vs. Sweeps and Magnetization per spin vs. Sweeps for the user-defined temperature on a 10x10 lattice, and includes  autocorrelation and error calculations.
`metropolis_gaussian.py` is where we started developing our sampling algorithm by first implementing a Metropolis-Hastings algorithm. We applied it to a probability density function of a standard normal distribution (mean = 0, standard deviation = 1) to compute the ratio of the probability of x and x + dx for a random point, then proposed a random step between -1 and 1.
`wolff_algo.py` was an additional method that we tested on a 2D 10x10 lattice. This file compares the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm to the Wolff algorithm for the same temperatures and using the same method of calculating autocorrelation. The motivation for this was to see how the two methods differed at temperatures near the estimated critical temperature.
